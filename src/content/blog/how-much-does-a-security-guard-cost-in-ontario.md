If you have never hired a security guard, the first question is always the same: **what is this going to cost?**

Here is the short answer. In 2026, a licensed security guard in Vaughan, Toronto and the rest of the GTA costs **$25 to $45 per hour**. Most jobs land in the middle, around $30 to $35.

The rest of this guide explains what moves the price up or down, so you can get a fair quote and not pay for things you do not need.

## The simple price table

| Type of work | Typical hourly rate (GTA, 2026) |
|---|---|
| Construction site gate guard | $25 – $32 |
| Warehouse or truck yard gate | $26 – $33 |
| Retail store floor guard | $27 – $35 |
| Condo lobby / concierge | $28 – $38 |
| Event security (weddings, banquets) | $30 – $40 |
| Hospital, clinic, care home | $32 – $42 |
| Supervisor or team lead | $38 – $45 |
| Executive protection | $45 and up |

These are **what you pay the company**, not what the guard takes home. The company pays the guard, plus insurance, licensing, uniforms, training, supervision and the dispatch desk that answers at 3 a.m.

## Six things that change the price

**1. The hours.** Overnight and weekend shifts cost a little more. Statutory holidays usually cost 1.5 times the normal rate, because the law says the guard must be paid that.

**2. How long you book.** One shift costs more per hour than a steady post. If you need a guard every night for six months, you will get a better rate than for one Saturday.

**3. The job itself.** Standing at a gate is simple. Running a condo front desk, handling residents, packages and complaints, takes a different kind of person. That costs a bit more.

**4. Training needed.** First aid, fire watch, de-escalation, or special site rules add to the rate.

**5. How fast you need it.** A guard tonight costs more than a guard next week. If you can plan ahead, do.

**6. The company's size.** Big national firms often charge more because they have more overhead. Small local companies can be cheaper but may struggle to cover a 24-hour post. A mid-size local company is usually the sweet spot.

## What should be included

A fair quote includes all of this. If it does not, ask why.

- A licensed, uniformed guard
- Supervisor checks during the shift
- A written report after every shift
- A dispatch desk you can call any time
- Liability insurance (ask for the certificate)
- No extra fee for the first site visit

## Cheaper options that work

A guard on site is not always the right answer. Two cheaper choices:

**Mobile patrol.** A marked car visits your property a few times a night. You pay **per visit**, often $20 to $40 each. Great for plazas, parking lots and empty buildings.

**Monitored cameras.** A real person watches your cameras and speaks through a loudspeaker if someone shows up. Priced **per month**. Great for large open sites like truck yards and car lots.

Many clients use a guard by day and cameras or patrol by night. That can cut the monthly bill in half.

## How to get a fair quote

1. **Tell the company the real hours.** "A few nights" is not enough. "Friday 6 p.m. to Monday 6 a.m., for eight weeks" gets you a real price.
2. **Ask for the hourly rate and the holiday rate.** Both, in writing.
3. **Ask what is in a shift report.** If they cannot show you a sample, that is a warning sign.
4. **Ask for the licence number and insurance certificate.** Every legal security company in Ontario has both. You can [check the licence online](/blog/how-to-check-if-a-security-company-is-licensed-in-ontario).
5. **Ask if there is a contract.** You should be able to start with a short booking.
