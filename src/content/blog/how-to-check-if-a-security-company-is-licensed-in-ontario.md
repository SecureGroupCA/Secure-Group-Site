In Ontario, a security guard is not just a person in a uniform. By law, every guard and every security company must hold a licence from the province. The law is called the **Private Security and Investigative Services Act**, or PSISA for short.

Hiring an unlicensed company puts you at risk. If something goes wrong, your insurer may refuse the claim. And the guard may not have the training to handle a real problem.

The good news: checking a licence takes about three minutes.

## Step 1: Ask for two numbers

Ask the company for:

1. **Their agency licence number.** This is the company's licence.
2. **The guard licence number** of any guard they send you.

A real company will give you both without any fuss. If they hesitate or say "we are working on it", stop there.

## Step 2: Look it up online

The Ontario government runs a free public lookup through ServiceOntario. You can search by licence number or by name.

- Go to the Ontario government website and search for "Private Security and Investigative Services licence lookup".
- Enter the licence number or the company name.
- The result shows whether the licence is **active**, when it expires, and what it covers (security guard, private investigator, or both).

The same lookup works for individual guards.

## Step 3: Check these four things

When the result comes up, look for:

- **Status is "Active."** Not expired, not suspended.
- **The name matches.** The company name on the licence should match the name on the quote and the invoice.
- **The licence type matches the work.** If they will do investigations, the licence must cover private investigation too, not just guarding.
- **The expiry date is in the future.** Licences are good for a set number of years and must be renewed.

## What a licensed company also has

Beyond the licence, a legal security company in Ontario must carry **general liability insurance**. Ask for the certificate. It should be current and name the company.

Licensed guards must also:

- Have passed a government-approved 40-hour training course and a provincial test
- Have passed a criminal background check
- Carry their licence while working
- Wear a uniform that follows the rules (for example, it cannot look like a police uniform)

## Warning signs

Walk away if you see any of these:

- "We do not need a licence for this kind of work." (In Ontario, they do.)
- Cash only, no invoice.
- Guards who cannot show a licence card.
- Uniforms with "Police" or "Enforcement" on them.
- No insurance certificate.
- A quote that is far below everyone else's. Someone is cutting a corner, and it is usually licensing, insurance or the guard's pay.

## Why this matters more than it sounds

If an unlicensed guard hurts someone or lets a theft happen, the person who hired them can be held responsible. Insurers look at this first. A licence is cheap protection for you.
